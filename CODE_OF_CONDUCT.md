# Code of conduct

Criticism of ideas is the point of this repository. Criticism of people is not. That is the
whole policy, and everything below is what it means in practice.

## What is expected

Be straightforward. Say the thing. A design document that nobody attacks is a design
document nobody read, and softening a real objection into a suggestion helps no one.

Assume good faith. Most disagreements here are about a language nobody has written much in
yet, so the odds that someone is wrong are high and the odds that they are arguing in bad
faith are low.

Argue against the strongest version of what someone said, not the weakest. If you are not
sure which version they meant, ask.

## What is not

Personal attacks, harassment, and pointed remarks about who someone is rather than what they
wrote. Repeatedly reopening a settled question without new information. Demanding
uncompensated work from people who owe you none.

None of that is a grey area, and none of it is worth the friction it costs.

## Scope

Anywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, commit messages, and any other space
where you are recognisably here on its behalf.

## If something goes wrong

Open an issue if the problem can be discussed in the open. Most can, and doing it in the
open is usually faster and fairer than doing it privately.

If it cannot, use GitHub's [report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) form,
which reaches both the maintainer and GitHub. There is no separate address to write to, and
inventing one that nobody monitors would be worse than saying so.

## What happens then

The maintainer will read it and respond. Depending on what happened, that means a comment,
an edit, a locked thread, or a block. There is one maintainer, so there is no committee and
no appeals process to describe. That is a real limitation of a project this size rather than
a policy choice, and pretending otherwise would be the kind of ceremony this project spends
its budget avoiding.
